Prague tips from Peter

A local's guide
to my Prague.

This is my city. I grew up here. If you're flying in for a meeting, a workshop, or a long weekend — these are the corners I actually send friends to. No tourist traps. No clichés. Just places I genuinely love.

01 · Coffee

Where I start my mornings.

Czech specialty coffee has come a long way in the last decade. These are the four spots I rotate between — depending on whether I want to work, talk, or just be quiet for an hour.

Dejvice
Kavárna Místo

The neighborhood spot of the Doubleshot family — quieter than Můj šálek, less rushed than Loka. There's a community table, a sofa, a small library nook, even stairs designed for quick coffee with your dog. The brunch is excellent. My favorite coffee stop when I'm moving around the centre — and where I land after my boxing training. The atmosphere is exactly right.

Bubenečská 12, Dejvice, Prague 6
Karlín
Loka Espresso Bar

Doubleshot's espresso bar inside the new Masaryčka building. Sleek interior, beautiful pastries, coffee that takes itself seriously without being pretentious. My pick when the coffee comes with a cake.

Ke Štvanici 371/4, Karlín, Prague 8
Karlín
Můj šálek kávy

Doubleshot's flagship in Karlín, and probably the most-named-checked specialty coffee place in Prague — for good reason. Brunch is excellent. Come early or expect a wait. My favorite stop when I'm in Karlín.

Křižíkova 105, Karlín, Prague 8
Old Town
Kavárna Slavia

Open since 1884. Kafka, Rilke, Havel and most of Czech literary history sat here. Skip the coffee snobbery for a morning — the real reason to come is the view across the river to Prague Castle. I never get tired of it. Order a hot chocolate.

Smetanovo nábřeží 1012/2, Old Town, Prague 1
02 · Food

Where I take friends.

Forget the goulash-and-dumplings tourist menus. The Prague food scene right now is honestly some of the best in Central Europe — modern, ingredient-led, unpretentious. Eight places I rotate between, depending on who's visiting and what we're celebrating.

Nové Město
Café Palanda

If you ask me where to find the best burger in Prague, this is it. No frills, no nonsense, just a small place a few minutes off the tourist track that does one thing extremely well.

Zlatnická 1122/11, Nové Město, Prague 1
Nové Město
Café Louvre

Open since 1902. Kafka, Einstein and Masaryk all had their tables here. The atmosphere is genuinely unique — and the prices are surprisingly fair for what you get. Come for the schnitzel, the Viennese-style breakfast, or just to sit upstairs and pretend it's still the First Republic. Billiards in the back if you want to make an evening of it.

Národní 22, Nové Město, Prague 1
Vinohrady
Čestr

Steakhouse from the Ambiente group, named after the Czech pied cattle breed. Dry-aged Czech beef, Pilsner from the tank, an open kitchen you can watch. My pick for a serious business dinner — the food does the talking. Reserve in advance — always.

Legerova 57/75, Vinohrady, Prague 1
Hradčany
Kuchyň

Czech home cooking, Ambiente-quality, in the Salm Palace right next to the castle. The kitchen brings the pots to your table — you lift the lids and pick what you want. Best terrace view in the city. My default lunch when I'm at Prague Castle.

Hradčanské náměstí 186/1, Hradčany, Prague 1
Dejvice
Tonkin

Vietnamese-Thai food the way it should be — no compromises for Czech palates. Phở with a clear, deep broth, real bún bò nam bộ, curries that actually have heat. I love Vietnamese food, and this is my top stop in Prague. The food is the star here, not the interior.

Na Hutích 661/9, Dejvice, Prague 6
Holešovice
Stejkárna

A no-frills steakhouse that punches well above its price. Aberdeen Angus from South America, US Black Angus, dry-aged properly, cooked exactly how you ask. Wood tables, dim lighting, no pretence. Where I go when I have something to celebrate.

Veletržní 246/5, Holešovice, Prague 7
Nové Město
Červený Jelen

The biggest restaurant in Prague, set inside Josef Gočár's cubist former Anglo-Czech Bank — reimagined by architect Stanislav Fiala. A 12-tank Pilsner tower stretching three floors, an Inferno wood grill, the original 800kg vault door turned into a regulars' table. The space genuinely fascinates me — bring a group, it's an event.

Hybernská 1034/5, Nové Město, Prague 1
Old Town
Brasileiro U Zelené žáby

Brazilian churrasco rodízio from Ambiente, set inside a 15th-century cellar a minute from Old Town Square. Passadores carve grilled meat directly onto your plate, salad bar with Nikkei sushi included, all you can eat for one price. Where I take family for the special occasions. Loud, generous, fun. Caipirinha required.

U Radnice 8/13, Old Town, Prague 1
03 · Beer

Beyond the tourist pub.

Czechs drink the most beer per capita in the world. That doesn't mean every pub knows how to pour it. Here are four that do — from a 180-year-old institution to a microbrewery from 2018.

Nové Město
U Pinkasů

The first place in Prague to serve Pilsner, back in 1843. Hrabal came every morning at 7 for goulash and a beer. Václav Havel decided to run for president over lunch here in December 1989. The Pilsner is still poured the way it was meant to be — and the atmosphere is something I genuinely love. Right in the centre, hard to beat.

Jungmannovo náměstí 16, Nové Město, Prague 1
Karlín
Dva Kohouti

A microbrewery and tap room under one roof — opened in 2018 by brewer Adam Matuška and Lukáš Svoboda from Lokál. Their own Místní ležák brewed in the morning, poured in the afternoon, plus rotating beers from other Czech microbreweries. Industrial space, DJs at night, a courtyard that's packed all summer. I come here for the vibe and the unusual beers.

Sokolovská 81/55, Karlín, Prague 8
Old Town
Lokál Dlouhááá

The original Lokál — and the longest one. 70 metres of bar, 12 tanks of Pilsner, 270 chairs, 1,500 guests a day. Ambiente runs it with their usual obsessive quality control. Order the smažák. The Czech classic that never disappoints. Don't be put off by the size; the beer is exactly the same.

Dlouhá 33, Old Town, Prague 1
Dejvice
Automat Matuška

The first dedicated taproom from Pivovar Matuška — one of my favorite Czech breweries, based in Broumy. Ten taps, a small experimental nano-brewery in the back, BBQ smoker out front. Right next to Hradčanská metro. This is where I take craft beer geeks.

Dejvická 4, Dejvice, Prague 6
04 · Nature

Green spaces I escape to.

Prague is one of the greenest capitals in Europe. Most visitors never leave the cobblestones. These four parks are where you'll find locals — running, picnicking, drinking wine, or just sitting under a tree.

Bubeneč
Stromovka

95 hectares of former royal hunting grounds — Prague's Central Park. Old trees, ponds, running paths, a planetarium, and a Renaissance summer palace on the hill. Easy 10 minutes from the city centre by tram, but it feels a world away. Where I run.

Královská obora, Bubeneč, Prague 7
Praha 6
Divoká Šárka

A wild rocky canyon at the edge of the city — hard to believe you're 20 minutes from Wenceslas Square. Hiking trails, an outdoor pool fed by a stream, sandstone cliffs. Take tram 26 to the end. Wear proper shoes; this isn't a stroll. The feeling of being in a real forest, ten minutes from my home and the airport.

Praha 6, Vokovice/Liboc
Vinohrady
Grébovka (Havlíčkovy sady)

The only working vineyard inside Prague city limits. Italian neoclassical garden, a grotto carved into the hillside, and a small wine pavilion where you can drink the wine grown 50 metres away. My favorite place in Prague for a date — sunset here in September is one of the city's best-kept secrets.

Havlíčkovy sady, Vinohrady, Prague 2
Liboc
Obora Hvězda

A Renaissance hunting park from 1530, laid out around a six-pointed star-shaped summer palace — yes, literally star-shaped. Quiet beech forest, straight allées, almost no tourists. Another piece of forest close to where I live. Insider tip: walk over to Sladká dílna at Na Petřinách 82 — a family-run patisserie ten minutes away. The cakes are worth the detour.

Liboc, Prague 6
05 · Skip these

What to avoid in my city.

Three tourist traps every Prague local wishes you'd skip — and what to do instead. Save your money for the places I actually recommend.

Street taxis
The classic Prague scam. A taxi waiting outside Old Town Square or Charles Bridge will quote you a fixed "tourist price" or run a creative meter. You'll pay 3–4× what locals pay. It happens to someone every single day.
✓ Use Uber or Bolt — fixed prices before you ride, drivers rated, works everywhere in the city. Bolt is usually cheaper.
Currency exchange offices
The "0% commission" boards in the centre? They give you 30–40% worse rates than your bank card. Especially avoid the standalone yellow ATMs (Euronet) — rates are significantly worse than bank ATMs. Same with the kiosks promising amazing rates near Wenceslas Square.
✓ Pay by card almost everywhere — restaurants, cafés, public transit. Use Revolut or your home bank card for best conversion. If you need cash, only use ATMs from major Czech banks: ČSOB, Komerční banka, Raiffeisenbank, Česká spořitelna.
Food stalls in the centre
The trdelník stands, sausage carts, and "traditional Czech food" stalls around Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square. Trdelník isn't even Czech (it's Hungarian-Slovak). Sausages are 4× the supermarket price. The "goulash in a bread bowl" is a tourist invention.
✓ Walk five minutes to one of the restaurants in the Food section above. Same money buys you actual Czech food cooked properly — and you'll sit down.
06 · Day trip

Out of the city, back by night.

Four trips out of Prague, from a 30-minute hop to a 3-hour train ride. The first two are easy day trips — leave by 9, back for dinner. The other two are technically doable in a day, but you'll regret rushing. Stay overnight if you can.

30 min by train
Karlštejn

A 14th-century Gothic castle built by Emperor Charles IV to keep the Bohemian crown jewels safe. The most photographed castle in the country for a reason. Take the train from Prague Smíchov, walk up the hill through the village, do the guided tour. Half a day, easily.

Karlštejn, Central Bohemia
1 hr by train
Kutná Hora

UNESCO-listed silver mining town, second only to Prague in the medieval Kingdom of Bohemia. Don't miss the Sedlec Ossuary — a chapel decorated with the bones of 40,000 people. Then cathedral, then lunch. Strange and beautiful.

Kutná Hora, Central Bohemia
3 hrs by train
Český Krumlov

A UNESCO Renaissance town built into a tight bend of the Vltava, dominated by an enormous baroque castle. Yes, it's far. Yes, it's worth it. Touristy in summer — go in shoulder season. If you can, stay one night; the town transforms after the day-trippers leave.

Český Krumlov, South Bohemia
2.5 hrs by train
Olomouc

A university town in Central Moravia that most foreigners skip — which is exactly why you should go. UNESCO Holy Trinity Column, an astronomical clock weirder than Prague's, excellent food, real student energy. Doable as a long day, but two days does it justice.

Olomouc, Central Moravia
07 · Getting around

How to actually move through Prague.

Skip the airport taxis and the random "city tour" cabs near tourist sites. Public transit here is excellent and cheap. A few practical things I tell every visiting friend.

From the airport, take MHD
Skip the taxis. Two great public-transit options from Václav Havel Airport (PRG) to the city center: Bus 59 connects to metro line A (Nádraží Veleslavín) — covered by a regular ticket. Or take the Airport Express (AE) bus straight to the main train station for 200 CZK — fast and direct.
💡 Bus 59 + metro takes ~35 min. AE takes ~30 min. Both beat sitting in airport-taxi traffic.
Never take a street taxi
Prague's classic tourist scam: a taxi waiting outside a famous sight. The meter starts at 200 CZK, the route gets creative, and you pay quadruple. Use Uber or Bolt instead. Both work everywhere in the city, prices are fixed before you ride, and drivers are rated. Bolt is usually a bit cheaper.
⚠️ If you absolutely have to take a street taxi, check that it's a verified company with a sign on the roof — and ask the price upfront.
Buy a 24-hour or 72-hour pass
One ticket, unlimited travel on metro, trams, and buses. 24 hours: 150 CZK (or 140 CZK in the app). 72 hours: 350 CZK (or 340 CZK in the app). Pays for itself after 4 rides. The Petřín funicular is included — bonus.
💡 Validate paper tickets in the yellow machine on first use — metro entrance, tram, or bus. Forgetting to validate = a 1,500 CZK fine. The app version validates automatically.
Get the PID Lítačka app
Prague's official transit app. Buy any ticket directly on your phone — about 10 CZK cheaper than paper, no validation needed, and you can buy mid-journey if you forgot. Works in English. The route planner is better than Google Maps for transit here.
💡 Search "PID Lítačka" in App Store or Google Play. Set it up before you leave the airport — saves a queue at the ticket machine.
